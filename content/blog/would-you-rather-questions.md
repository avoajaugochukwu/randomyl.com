> Would you rather is a forced-choice game: someone reads a two-option dilemma and everyone has to pick one, because [answering "neither" or "both" is against the rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Would_you_rather). The fun is in the debate that follows. Below are 200+ questions sorted by mood, so you can grab the right kind fast.

The whole game lives or dies on the quality of the questions. A flat one gets a shrug. A good one gets an argument, a laugh, or a "wait, I need to think about that." This page is built to be read out loud, so it is split into clear categories. Jump to the vibe you want, pick a few, and make people commit.

One rule keeps it fun: no "neither," no "both," no weaseling. As Wikipedia puts it for the classic version of the game, the dilemma can be between [two good options, two attractive choices, or two bad ones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Would_you_rather) and the player has to land somewhere. That forced choice is the point. The moment someone has to defend a ridiculous answer, you have a game.

## How to play, briefly

There aren't many rules, which is why it travels so well to road trips, classrooms, and parties.

- One person reads a question. Everyone answers, and you can't pick "neither" or "both."
- Go around the circle and make each person explain their choice. The reasons are funnier than the answers.
- Mix the categories. A couple of gross ones, then a deep one, keeps the energy moving.
- If a question splits the room, let people argue it out before moving on.

If your group likes the dares more than the dilemmas, the same energy works for [truth or dare questions](/blog/truth-or-dare-questions), which pair nicely with this game on the same night.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the rules of would you rather?

One person reads a two-option question and everyone has to choose one. The only real rule is that you [can't answer "neither" or "both"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Would_you_rather). After everyone picks, the fun part is making each person explain why, which usually turns into a friendly debate.

### What is a good would you rather question?

A good one offers two options that are roughly equal, so the choice is genuinely hard. The two options can both be good, both be bad, or one of each. If everyone instantly picks the same answer, the question is too easy. The best ones split the room or make people stop and think.

### How many would you rather questions are on this page?

More than 200, sorted into funny, hard, deep, kids, adults, gross, food, superpower, and tasteful 18+ categories. The idea is that you can jump straight to the mood your group wants instead of scrolling through one long mixed list.

### Are would you rather questions good for kids?

Yes, with a little curation. Stick to the kids and food categories, set a boundary on how gross things get, and the game becomes a great way to spark laughter and imagination. It works well in classrooms, car rides, and at family dinners.
